Navigation across the four PlasmaDesign staff apps

One SSO session reaches Proofs, vCard Studio, Card Programme and Stock Control. The cross-app strip that ties them together works — it lists exactly the apps a person can reach and gets them there in one click. What follows is why it does not yet look like one product, and two ways to fix that.

Findings are drawn from the code, not the screenshots: appSwitcher.ts (identical in all four repos), DesignerHeader.tsx, AppShell.tsx, App.tsx, Dashboard.jsx and design-tokens.css.

One decision I made for you

You left the convergence question open, so: the chrome becomes one shared component, not four standardised ones. Nine findings out of nine are drift, and drift is what copied code does. That means accepting a build-time dependency the current module deliberately avoids — the honest fallback is to keep copying verbatim and make the copy the whole chrome instead of just the strip, which costs you a four-repo diff every time it changes.

What it buys

Fifty pixels back, one brand statement, and no ambiguity about layers because there is only one. The app menu is the only control on the bar that names a place outside the current app, so "am I changing app or page" answers itself by position.

What it costs

Switching app is two clicks instead of one, and the other three apps are no longer visible at rest. If a shift genuinely hops between Proofs and Stock all day, this is the wrong trade and 1b is your answer.

1b

Two layers, one component

{{ heightB }} of chrome

Keep one-click access to all four apps, but stop pretending the strip is part of the app. It goes ink-dark: unmistakably a level above, no tab shapes, no cream on cream, and the current app marked by a coral underline rather than a folder-tab cut-out.

What it buys

One-click app switching survives, and the layer question is settled by contrast rather than by a folder-tab illusion that needed all four headers to be the same white. Ink also gives the estate somewhere to put account role and, later, environment banners.

What it costs

Still two bars, still 92px. And a dark band above a warm cream product is a strong statement — it will read as a tool rather than as a studio, which may or may not be what you want staff to feel all day.

05  Both, as a per-person preference

Yes, and it costs almost nothing — because the two directions are not two components. 1b is 1a with one extra layer switched on. The bar, the nav, the right cluster and the mobile treatment are identical in both; the only difference is whether the ink strip is present and whether the app name carries a chevron.

So the preference is one boolean, and the control is one row in the account menu. Try it on the bar below — the toggle lives in the account menu at the right, exactly where it would in production.

What a preference does cost you

Every screen now has two chrome heights, so anything sticky below it has to read the offset from the component rather than assume a constant. Stock Control's filter bar and Proofs' sticky admin header both do exactly that today, with hardcoded values.

And support gets slightly harder: "the bar at the top" now describes two things. Worth it for two people switching every few hours, but it is the reason I would not add a third option later.

06  What changes, per repo

Identical for both directions. The switcher module stops being the shared thing and the whole chrome becomes it.

Shared by package, not by copy

The copy-verbatim rule was right for a strip and stops being right for a chrome.

Look at what has actually drifted. The one file that is copied verbatim has not: four identical copies of appSwitcher.ts, no divergence. Everything that was never shared — the four headers — has drifted into nine findings. So copying is not the disease here; not sharing is.

But the rule that makes the copy safe is that the file is presentational, dependency-free and stateless. A chrome is not: it needs router-aware links, a controlled search input, focus handling in two popovers, a bottom sheet, and — once you add the preference — a cookie contract. Written as framework-free DOM-building that would be several times the size and worse than what each app has today. And a preference is behaviour, so four copies of it is where drift stops being cosmetic: one app reading a differently-named cookie means a person's setting silently fails to follow them, and nothing looks broken.

Recommendation

Build 1a, ship the strip from 1b as a layer over it, default it on.

You told me the tab strip, the four nav idioms, the four account treatments, the doubled wordmark and the cream-on-cream all bother you. Both directions fix all five, because the fix for all five is the same shared component — which is also why the preference in section 05 is affordable rather than a second build.

Default it on, for two reasons. Your own answer — a couple of people crossing over every few hours — and the code agreeing with them: the proof viewer deep-links into the vCard studio, the studio deep-links into proof jobs, and the programme console renders the switcher even for people who hold no role there specifically so they can get back out. Someone who wants the clean bar turns it off once and never thinks about it again; someone who needs the strip and cannot find it loses time every few hours.
